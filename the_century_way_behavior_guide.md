# The Century Way

## Girls Volleyball Player Standards Guide & Team Agreement

### Century High School Girls Volleyball

---

## Purpose of This Guide

The Century Way explains the behavioral standards for Century Girls Volleyball.

Every student who participates in tryouts will receive access to this guide. Players who are selected for a team will be required to review it and complete the digital agreement before participating in the jamboree or any team competition.

This is not meant to be a rule sheet or a punishment document. It is meant to help every player understand what is expected, why those expectations matter, and how the coaching staff will help players meet those expectations.

Signing this agreement does not mean you are expected to be perfect. It means you understand the standards of the program and are willing to work toward them.

Our goal is to build a volleyball environment where players can compete, learn, grow, and belong. That requires skill, effort, communication, responsibility, and respect.

By accepting a place in this program, you are agreeing to act in ways that help yourself, your teammates, and the team get better.

---

# Our Core Standard

**The Century Way means we act in ways that help ourselves, our teammates, and our team get better.**

That standard applies in practice, matches, classrooms, hallways, team events, online spaces, and anywhere we represent Century Volleyball.

You are not expected to be perfect.

You are expected to be:

- Honest
- Coachable
- Respectful
- Responsible
- Willing to grow

When you are unsure what to do, ask yourself:

**Is this helping me, my teammates, or our team get better?**

If the answer is no, notice it, adapt, and commit to a better next action.

---

# The Seven Standards of The Century Way

1. Be Present
2. Respect the Team Environment
3. Compete With Courage
4. Communicate Honestly and Respectfully
5. Take Responsibility
6. Support Your Teammates
7. Represent Century Well

---

# 1. Be Present

## What This Means

Being present means you are physically, mentally, and emotionally ready to participate.

This includes:

- Arriving on time
- Being dressed and prepared
- Putting away distractions
- Listening when others are speaking
- Giving honest effort
- Staying engaged even when you are tired, frustrated, or not playing
- Being ready to contribute in whatever role you have that day

## Why It Matters

Your presence affects the people around you.

When you are locked in, your teammates feel it. When you are checked out, they feel that too.

A team cannot grow if players are only present when things are fun, easy, or going their way.

Being present does not mean you always feel great. It means you are willing to show up honestly and do the best you can with where you are that day.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Creating clear practice plans
- Explaining what we are working on and why
- Giving reminders and resets
- Helping players refocus without shame
- Checking in when a player seems disconnected
- Talking with players when disengagement becomes a pattern

---

# 2. Respect the Team Environment

## What This Means

Respect is shown through actions, not just words.

This includes:

- Speaking to teammates, coaches, officials, opponents, and staff appropriately
- Taking care of equipment and facilities
- Avoiding gossip, exclusion, eye-rolling, side conversations, and negative body language
- Treating every teammate as someone who belongs
- Handling conflict directly and maturely
- Understanding that your behavior affects the group

## Why It Matters

Teams do not fall apart only because of big problems. They often fall apart through small repeated behaviors that make people feel unsafe, unwanted, or unimportant.

Respect protects the environment so everyone can learn, compete, and grow.

A respectful team environment does not mean everyone is best friends. It means every player can trust that they will be treated with basic dignity and fairness.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Addressing disrespect early
- Naming behaviors clearly
- Supporting players through conflict
- Teaching better ways to communicate
- Holding everyone to the same team standard
- Protecting the learning environment for all players

---

# 3. Compete With Courage

## What This Means

Competing with courage means you are willing to try, fail, learn, and try again.

This includes:

- Going for the ball
- Communicating loudly and clearly
- Taking smart risks
- Responding well after mistakes
- Staying aggressive under pressure
- Not hiding when the game gets hard
- Choosing commitment over fear

## Why It Matters

Volleyball is a game of mistakes.

Players who are afraid to make mistakes stop learning. Players who can recover quickly become more valuable to the team.

We do not need perfect players. We need players who can stay committed after imperfect moments.

Courage is not about always feeling confident. Courage means you are willing to act with purpose even when you feel nervous, frustrated, or unsure.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Designing practices where mistakes are part of learning
- Teaching “Next Point” responses
- Rewarding brave decisions, not just successful outcomes
- Helping players understand what to notice and adjust
- Giving players chances to grow through challenge
- Supporting players as they learn to handle pressure

---

# 4. Communicate Honestly and Respectfully

## What This Means

Communication is a responsibility.

This includes:

- Telling coaches when something is wrong
- Asking questions when confused
- Speaking directly instead of complaining sideways
- Using a tone that keeps conversations productive
- Being honest about injuries, absences, concerns, and conflicts
- Listening when others are trying to communicate with you

## Why It Matters

Coaches cannot help with problems they do not know about. Teammates cannot trust each other if communication happens through gossip, silence, or passive-aggressive behavior.

Healthy communication keeps small issues from becoming big ones.

Communication does not have to be perfect, but it does need to be honest and respectful.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Listening before judging
- Asking questions
- Making space for player voice
- Helping players prepare for hard conversations
- Being clear about decisions, roles, and expectations when possible
- Encouraging players to communicate early instead of waiting until frustration builds

---

# 5. Take Responsibility

## What This Means

Taking responsibility means owning your choices without blaming, avoiding, or making excuses.

This includes:

- Admitting mistakes
- Accepting feedback
- Making things right when needed
- Following through on commitments
- Being honest about your effort and attitude
- Understanding that your choices affect the team
- Looking for what you can control

## Why It Matters

Trust is built when people do what they say they will do.

A team becomes stronger when players can own their part and move forward.

Responsibility is not about punishment. It is about maturity.

Everyone makes mistakes. The question is what you do next.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Giving clear feedback
- Separating the person from the behavior
- Helping players identify better choices
- Providing chances to repair trust
- Following up when patterns need to change
- Holding players accountable while still supporting their growth

---

# 6. Support Your Teammates

## What This Means

Supporting teammates means you help create an environment where others can compete, struggle, and grow.

This includes:

- Encouraging teammates after mistakes
- Celebrating others’ success
- Giving useful information
- Avoiding comparison and jealousy
- Being a good teammate whether you are playing or not
- Making the team better from any role
- Helping teammates stay connected to the next point

## Why It Matters

Every player wants to play. Not every player will have the same role.

A strong team is built when players can still contribute even when their role is hard, unclear, or different than they hoped.

Your value to the team is bigger than your playing time.

Great teammates make the people around them better.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Clarifying roles when needed
- Recognizing contributions beyond stats
- Helping players handle disappointment
- Teaching athletes how to be useful from the bench, sideline, and practice court
- Building team activities that require connection and cooperation
- Helping players understand that support is an active skill

---

# 7. Represent Century Well

## What This Means

You represent this program in more places than the court.

This includes:

- Classroom behavior
- Social media
- Bus rides
- Locker rooms
- Home and away matches
- Communication with officials and opponents
- How you treat people when coaches are not watching

## Why It Matters

Your actions shape how people see you, your teammates, your coaches, your school, and our program.

Reputation is built through repeated behavior.

Representing Century well does not mean pretending to be perfect. It means understanding that your choices matter because you are part of something bigger than yourself.

## How Coaches Will Help

Coaches will help by:

- Being clear about what representation looks like
- Addressing behavior that harms the program
- Teaching players how to repair mistakes
- Holding players accountable to the standard
- Modeling the behavior we ask from athletes
- Helping players understand the connection between personal choices and team culture

---

# When Expectations Are Not Being Met

If a player is not meeting the team standard, coaches will respond based on the situation and the pattern.

Possible coach responses may include:

- A quick reminder
- A private conversation
- A reset or removal from an activity
- A role adjustment
- A parent/guardian conversation
- A team accountability plan
- Referral to school support when needed
- Loss of team privileges when behavior continues

The goal is not to punish players. The goal is to protect the team environment and help players grow.

However, support does not remove accountability. If behavior continues after support, consequences may become necessary.

---

# Repairing Mistakes

Every player will fall short at some point.

The Century Way is not about never making mistakes. It is about learning how to respond when mistakes happen.

When a player falls short, the expected response is:

1. **Notice** what happened.
2. **Own** the behavior without blaming others.
3. **Repair** what needs to be repaired.
4. **Commit** to a better next action.

Repair might include:

- Apologizing
- Changing behavior
- Having a direct conversation
- Rebuilding trust over time
- Asking for help
- Following through consistently

A mistake does not have to define you. Repeated behavior does.

---

# Our Commitment as Coaches

As coaches, we will work to:

- Be clear
- Be consistent
- Be respectful
- Be honest
- Listen to players
- Help players grow
- Address problems early
- Create an environment where players can compete, learn, and belong

We will not be perfect. When we make mistakes, we will work to own them and improve.

We ask players to do the same.

---

# Daily Check-In Connection

During the season, we will use a daily check-in system to help players connect these standards to practice.

Before practice, players may be asked to:

- Report how they are doing physically
- Report how they are doing mentally
- Report how they are doing emotionally
- Choose one Century Way standard to focus on
- Set a daily goal connected to that standard

After practice, players may be asked to:

- Reflect on how they did with their goal
- Identify what helped or got in the way
- Name something they want to remember for the next practice

The purpose of the check-in system is support, not surveillance.

It helps coaches understand where players are, what they need, and how we can better support the whole person.

The Century Way defines the standard.

The daily check-in helps players live it day to day.
